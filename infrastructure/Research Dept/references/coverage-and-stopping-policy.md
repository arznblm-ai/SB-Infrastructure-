# Coverage and Stopping Policy

### [[2026-04-14]]

## Coverage sufficiency rule

Research is not complete because the document is long. Research is complete only when coverage is materially sufficient for the request.

## Coverage dimensions

### 1. Question Coverage

- all key research questions materially addressed;
- unresolved questions explicitly listed.

### 2. Source-Type Coverage

- primary sources included when relevant and available;
- analytical sources included when interpretation matters;
- field signal sources included when early pattern detection matters.

### 3. Perspective Coverage

When relevant, scan for:

- vendor perspective;
- user perspective;
- operator perspective;
- critic or skeptical perspective.

### 4. Time Coverage

When relevant, cover:

- historical baseline;
- current state;
- newest developments.

### 5. Counterevidence Coverage

- one dominant narrative is not enough;
- a research run cannot be marked complete if it has only one narrative and no counterevidence scan.

## Cross-verification rules

### Strong factual claim

Examples:

- numbers;
- launch dates;
- pricing;
- market size;
- growth;
- user counts;
- feature existence;
- specific event.

Requirement:

- minimum two independent confirmations where possible;
- or one strong primary source;
- confidence must be marked.

### Interpretive claim

Examples:

- market matured;
- category overheated;
- this changes workflow;
- users are tired of X.

Requirement:

- multiple supporting signals;
- preferably across different source types;
- must be marked as interpretation, not fact.

### Emerging weak-signal claim

Examples:

- a new wave may be forming;
- early users are starting to adopt X;
- field interest around Y appears to be rising.

Requirement:

- must be marked as weak signal;
- cannot be collapsed into strong conclusion;
- must remain clearly labeled as weak signal and not be treated as established factual evidence.

## Formal stopping policy

Research may stop only if:

1. Research questions are materially covered.
2. Source diversity is sufficient for this topic.
3. Key claims have been verified to an appropriate degree.
4. Main contradictions or tensions have been identified.
5. Additional searching produces diminishing returns.
6. Known unknowns are explicitly listed.
7. Confidence boundaries are stated.

## Diminishing returns threshold

Definition:

- search may stop when new sources mostly repeat already-covered evidence or narratives;
- but only after the minimum quality floor has been reached.

Minimum quality floor:

- source hierarchy respected;
- key claims verified;
- counterevidence scan completed;
- uncertainty explicit.
