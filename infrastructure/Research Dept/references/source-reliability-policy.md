# Source Reliability Policy

## Core rule

Department обязан предпочитать силу источника удобству поиска.

Additional mix rules:

- Official sources explain mechanics. Authoritative external sources explain the market.
- Any serious market-level research must include not only primary platform/company sources, but also high-quality external market-overview sources.

## Source hierarchy

### Tier 1A: Company / Platform Primary Sources

Examples:

- official documentation
- official company websites
- product pages
- changelogs
- research papers
- regulatory filings
- earnings calls
- direct interviews with founders, operators, creators
- official datasets
- original reports
- original speeches, talks, transcripts
- first-hand releases

Policy:

- strongest evidence basis;
- preferred wherever available for platform mechanics, product facts, pricing, policy constraints, and formal company claims;
- may support strong factual claims directly;
- not sufficient on their own for category-level market overview, leader mapping, or market-evolution claims.

### Tier 1B: Authoritative Market Overview Sources

Examples:

- strong independent journalism
- reputable business and technology press
- analyst-style market reporting
- trade publications with sourcing discipline
- deep category explainers
- high-quality market reports
- reporting-oriented industry newsletters

Policy:

- required when the research asks for market overview, category understanding, category evolution, leader mapping, or market growth narratives;
- provide external perspective on how the category is described beyond vendor self-presentation;
- should be used alongside Tier 1A, not instead of Tier 1A.

### Tier 2: Specialist Secondary Sources

Examples:

- reputable industry analysis
- serious expert essays
- specialist newsletters with strong track record
- long-form analysis by domain experts
- quality media with clear sourcing
- independent technical reviews with evidence

Policy:

- valid for interpretation and synthesis;
- should not fully replace Tier 1A when Tier 1A exists and is accessible.

### Tier 3: Field Signal / Weak Signal Sources

Examples:

- X or Twitter threads
- Reddit discussions
- comment sections
- YouTube commentary
- user communities
- forums
- creator hot takes
- public ad library entries
- search-result ad snippets
- social posts and public profile updates
- marketplace/review snippets
- informal posts

Policy:

- useful for pain points, field language, early signal discovery, observed messaging, CTA, offer, creative-format, and ICP signal discovery when the claim is explicitly framed as observed public signal;
- not sufficient basis for strong factual claims without stronger confirmation.

### Advertising and ICP signal policy

Public ads, landing pages, social posts, search snippets, directories, and profile pages are valid evidence for what a company publicly says or shows. They are not, by themselves, proof that a message performs well, that a campaign is profitable, or that a company is a high-priority lead.

For competitive messaging runs:

- label direct observations separately from inferred strategy;
- use date/capture notes because ads and landing pages change quickly;
- prefer official ad libraries, company pages, archived captures, public creative centers, and platform transparency tools when available;
- do not infer spend, ROAS, conversion, or internal intent unless a credible source states it.

For account/ICP runs:

- use only public, sourceable signals unless the user provides private data or an installed connector explicitly supplies it;
- separate fit signals from disqualifying signals;
- treat hiring, funding, tech stack, public customers, and marketplace footprints as proxies, not proof;
- do not fabricate emails, personal data, or private outreach intelligence.

### Tier 4: Derived / Low-Trust Sources

Examples:

- AI-generated summaries without provenance
- SEO blogs with weak sourcing
- generic content farms
- rehosted summaries
- vague top-10 pages without evidence
- unattributed claims
- scraped content with unclear origin

Policy:

- cannot be used as evidence basis;
- may be used only as navigation clue to stronger original materials.

## Source selection rules

1. Prefer highest available source tier.
2. Do not confuse popularity with reliability.
3. Use Tier 3 for signal, not for proof.
4. Mark source strength explicitly on every source card.
5. Track dependency concentration when too many findings rely on one source, one vendor or one narrative.
6. If the run makes market-level claims, include authoritative external overview coverage.
7. Do not infer market leaders, market growth, or category evolution from company self-description alone.

## Required source-card annotations

Each normalized source card must include:

- source tier;
- source role;
- internal or external perspective marker;
- trust rationale;
- limitations;
- whether it can support facts, interpretations or only signals;
- freshness note;
- dependency concentration note when relevant.
